## How the curriculum is built

- **Approach:** CEFR action-oriented. Modes: Spoken reception, Written reception, Spoken production, Written production, Spoken interaction, Written interaction, Mediation.
- **Competences:** Linguistic, Sociolinguistic, Pragmatic, Phonological control, Orthographic control, Spoken fluency.

| Curriculum level | CEFR | Older name | Grades | Vocabulary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-Basic User | Pre-A1 | Pre-Foundation | Grade 3 and/or first months of grade 4 | Pre-Band I (~200 words) |
| Basic User I | A1 | Foundation | Grade 4 → end of grade 6 | Band I Core I (grade 4 → mid-5), Core II (mid-5 → end of 6); Grammar Band I |

**End of grade 6 = CEFR A1**, about **1,400 receptive / 1,200 productive words** (approximate targets).

## Grade by grade

### Grades 1–2 — oral/aural exposure
- English is mandatory from grade 3 (State Comptroller report, July 2024).
- **Enjoy English**: oral/aural pre-foundation program for preschool through grade 2 — songs and themes (greetings, colours, numbers, feelings, animals, family, body, clothes, actions, transport, weather, food).
- Focus: listening and speaking only, no formal literacy.
- *Example for the game:* "Touch the red balloon!" — the child hears it and taps; no reading needed.

### Grade 3 — Pre-Foundation / Pre-A1
- Can-do: understand short instructions ("Close the door"), greet and give name, ask "What is this?", say how they feel.
- Literacy: phonological awareness (rhyme, syllables, blending), letter–sound links then digraphs, letter formation, decode/spell CVC and CV words, read and spell the ~200 Pre-Band words.
- Pre-Band I includes many chunks: *What's your name? How old are you? I like … / I don't like … Do you have …? I live in …*
- *Example for the game:* "Sound Hunt" — hear /k/ /a/ /t/, drag the letters c-a-t, see the cat picture appear.

### Grade 4 — entering Foundation / A1 (pilot grade)
- Start of year: finish Pre-Band I, then Band I Core I.
- Mikud goals: understand concrete everyday info spoken slowly; **give factual personal information**; read short stories/texts on familiar topics with pictures; write simple phrases and sentences (developmental spelling allowed); reading fluency.
- A1 can-do: describe yourself, family, where you live, appearance and clothes, where objects are, likes and dislikes; write about yourself.
- Literacy: blend and decode at word/chunk level; read familiar irregular words; digraphs sh, ch, th.
- Grammar introduced (Grammar Band I — verify against PDF): *be* (statements, questions, there is/are), present simple (habits, negative, Yes/No with *do*), *can/can't*, imperatives and *Let's*, regular past affirmative, present continuous affirmative, *and/or/but/because*, plurals, articles, possessives and *'s*, pronouns, prepositions of time and place.
- *Example sentences at this level:* "My name is Noa. I am nine. I have a brother. I like cats but I don't like dogs."

### Grade 5 — A1 (Core I → Core II)
- Adds: past of *be*, irregular past, past negatives and questions with *did*, present continuous questions, *will* / *going to*, frequency adverbs, wh- questions, comparatives (-er), quantifiers.
- ABLE Kit literacy screening early in the year.
- *Example:* "Yesterday I went to the park. Did you go too?"

### Grade 6 — end of Foundation (A1)
- Adds: superlatives, comparatives with *more … than*, adverbs of manner, *will* wh- questions.
- External Tnufa exam (May 2027 per the 2026–27 bulletin).
- *Example:* "The elephant is the biggest animal in the zoo."

## Suggested grade-4 unit topics (fit Band I)
The lexical bands are alphabetical, not topical — topics below are a grouping of Band I words with the A1 can-do statements.

1. **All About Me / My Family** — be, possessives, have. *"This is my mom. Her name is Dana."* ← **recommended pilot**: matches the grade-4 goal "give factual personal information" almost word for word and builds on chunks the child already knows.
2. **My Day** — present simple, time. *"I get up at seven."*
3. **Food I Like** — I like / I don't like, plurals. *"I like bananas. Do you like eggs?"*
4. **Animals** — can/can't, there is/are. *"A fish can swim. It can't fly."*
5. **My House / Where Is It?** — prepositions. *"The cat is under the chair."*
6. **Clothes and Weather** — present continuous. *"She is wearing a red hat."*
7. **Jobs and People** — *"He is a doctor."*
