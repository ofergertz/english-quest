# The games that should teach best

Chosen from the research in [`learning-science.md`](learning-science.md) and [`apps-survey.md`](apps-survey.md). Each game below is built on a mechanic with real evidence (strength noted), tailored to a Hebrew-speaking child, grades 1–6 with grade 4 as the pilot. **Every game has a worked example and a positive-feedback line.** Names are working titles; characters and art must be original.

Legend — evidence: ●●● strong · ●●○ moderate · ●○○ weak/untested

## 1. Sound Sorter — hear it, tap it ●●●
- **Does:** hears "banana", taps the right picture out of 2–4; then sees the word printed (grade 3+).
- **Why:** retrieval practice with immediate corrective feedback is the strongest, best-supported mechanic.
- **Example round (unit "My Family"):** audio "sister" → pictures: sister / brother / mom / dad.
- **Wrong tap:** correct picture glows, "sister" replays: *"Almost! This is a sister. Listen: sister."*
- **Right tap:** *"You heard it! That's a sister."*
- **Grades:** 1–6 (fewer choices and no print for 1–2).

## 2. Word Builder — letters to word ●●●
- **Does:** hears "sun" with sounds /s/ /u/ /n/; drags letter tiles in order; the word is blended aloud as the tiles slide together.
- **Why:** systematic phonics (d = 0.41; 0.55 if early); letter-sound practice fills a known gap in Israeli textbooks.
- **Example:** target *mom* → tiles m, o, m, plus one distractor (a). Each placed tile plays its sound.
- **Positive:** *"You built *mom* all by yourself — m-o-m!"*
- **Grades:** 3–4 core; grades 5–6 with longer words and digraphs (*sh, ch, th*).

## 3. Sound Hunt — which word starts with…? ●●○
- **Does:** audio only, no print: "Which word starts with /s/? sun, map, sit".
- **Why:** phonemic awareness for non-readers; works with blending/segmenting.
- **Example:** hear *sun / map / sit* → child picks the two that start with /s/.
- **Positive:** *"Your ears are sharp — sun and sit both start with /s/!"*
- **Grades:** 1–3.

## 4. Do-It Scenes — listen and do ●●○
- **Does:** hears "Put the red ball under the table", drags objects into the scene. Grades 1–3 add a physical action ("Jump!" — the guide jumps, the child jumps too).
- **Why:** listening-first input and TPR/gesture build vocabulary without forcing speech; gestures matched pictures for children (not better).
- **Example:** scene with a table, chair, red and blue balls: "Put the blue ball on the chair."
- **Positive:** *"You understood the whole sentence!"*

## 5. Minimal-Pair Detective — which did you hear? ●●○ (older kids)
- **Does:** hears *ship* or *sheep*; taps the matching picture. Pairs chosen for Hebrew interference.
- **Why:** Hebrew has 5 vowels and no th/short-long contrast; Building Blocks flags *feel/fill, bad/bed, b/p, v/w*.
- **Example:** *three / tree*, *west / vest*, *bad / bed*, *ship / sheep*. After a miss both words are shown side by side: "sh-i-p (short) · sh-ee-p (long)".
- **Positive:** *"You caught the tiny difference — that's detective work!"*
- **Rule:** introduce confusables on different days first, contrast later.
- **Grades:** 4–6.

## 6. Memory Garden — spaced review ●●●
- **Does:** a mixed daily review: each word is a plant that "wants water" when it is due; the child waters it by answering a short retrieval (tap/type/spell).
- **Why:** spacing across days is among the strongest effects; schedule: same session → next day → ~3 days → ~1 week → 2–3 weeks → monthly.
- **Example:** *apple* (learned Sunday) returns Monday, Wednesday, next Sunday. Plants only grow — nothing ever wilts or dies.
- **Positive:** *"Your garden grew 6 new flowers today — 18 words you know!"*
- **Grades:** all.

## 7. Sentence Builder — word tiles to sentence ●●○
- **Does:** drags word tiles into order (English LTR) to make a sentence; sentence is read aloud on success.
- **Why:** moves from words to productive grammar (A1 *be*, possessives, present simple); dual coding with a picture.
- **Example (grade 4):** tiles *is / name / My / Dana / mom's* → "My mom's name is Dana."
- **Positive:** *"A whole sentence! You're writing English."*
- **Hint after 2 misses:** first tile appears in place; never a failure screen.

## 8. Story Theater — decodable mini-story ●●○
- **Does:** reads/listens to a 3–6 sentence story built only from taught words; taps any word to hear it; answers one comprehension question.
- **Why:** reading while listening gave the best delayed retention among input modes; ties units together with narrative.
- **Example (grade 4):** "This is my family. I have a mom and a dad. I have one sister. We like pizza." → Q: *Who is in the family?* (pick pictures).
- **Positive:** *"You read a whole story!"*

## 9. Say-It Mirror — record and compare ●○○
- **Does:** hears a model, records the word, hears own recording next to the model. **No score, no blocking.**
- **Why:** speaking matters, but child ASR is wrong 15–25% of the time, so self-comparison is safer. Optional ASR only for known target words with forgiving thresholds.
- **Example:** target *three* — the child hears: model · own recording · model.
- **Positive:** *"You said it! Listen — can you hear the 'th'?"*
- **Grades:** 4–6.

## 10. Tricky Word Creatures — sight words ●●○
- **Does:** words that cannot be sounded out (*the, said, you, are*) are friendly creatures; the child "feeds" or tickles the right one when its word is heard or shown.
- **Why:** sight-word automaticity is a curriculum requirement; keeps decodable and tricky words clearly separate (a confusion in some products).
- **Example:** hear "said" → tap the creature wearing *said* among *said / sad / and*.
- **Timer:** optional and self-referenced only, for mastered words.

## 11. b/d Compass — reversals ●○○ (inference, no Hebrew-specific study)
- **Does:** picks the right letter using an anchor: **b**ed — the bed's *b* faces the headboard on the left; a left-to-right sweep highlights the word while decoding.
- **Why:** writing direction contributes to reversals; the child reads right-to-left in Hebrew.
- **Example:** hear "dog" → drag *d* or *b* to complete _og.
- **Positive:** *"You checked the direction — smart reading!"*

## 12. Chat with a Character — guided dialogue ●●○
- **Does:** a mascot asks a question; the child chooses a reply (audio + picture + text). Later rounds ask for a typed or recorded answer.
- **Why:** A1 can-do "give factual personal information" and spoken interaction.
- **Example:** Owl: *"What's your name?"* → [My name is ___] · *"How old are you?"* → [I am nine.] · *"Do you have a sister?"* → [Yes, I do.] / [No, I don't.]
- **Positive:** *"That was a real English conversation!"*

## 13. Teach the Owl — the child teaches the mascot ●○○ (design idea, no direct evidence found)
- **Does:** the mascot "forgets" a word; the child picks the right picture and the owl "learns" it. Adds purpose to retrieval.
- **Example:** Owl: "I forgot… what is *brother*?" → child taps the brother picture.
- **Positive:** *"The owl learned *brother* from you — thank you, teacher!"*

## Daily session recipe (12–15 minutes)
1. **Warm-up (2 min)** — Memory Garden (due words).
2. **Discover (3–4 min)** — new words/pattern: picture + audio + (print), repeat after the model.
3. **Practice (5 min)** — two different games from the list (e.g. Sound Sorter + Word Builder).
4. **Use it (3 min)** — Sentence Builder, Chat with a Character, or Story Theater.
5. **Reward (1 min)** — a story page or album sticker; "great stopping point".

Target about 80–85% success; mastery gate ~80–90% across separate sessions; ≤10–12 new words per session.

## What not to build
Shop/currency · leaderboards · speed-scored ranking · lives/hearts · streak-shaming notifications · always-on decorative animation · bare "Wrong!" messages · speech-recognition gating.
